# Week 4 LLM Usage Report

## Overview

During Week 4, the team used Large Language Models (LLMs), mainly ChatGPT, as a supporting tool for documentation, planning, and report preparation. The LLM was not used as an independent source of truth. All outputs created with LLM support were reviewed and corrected by team members before being included in the project documentation or submitted as part of the coursework.

## Areas Where LLM Was Used

### Report Structure and Checklists

LLM assistance was used to break down the Assignment 4 requirements into smaller actionable tasks. This helped the team understand which documents had to be prepared for Week 4, including the public report, customer review summary, UAT documentation, retrospective, reflection, and LLM usage report.

The LLM was also used to create checklists for missing report sections and to organize the work in a more systematic order.

### Customer Review Documentation

The team used LLM support to prepare a cleaner version of the customer review materials. This included transforming meeting notes and transcript fragments into a more readable summary while preserving the actual meaning of the customer feedback.

The LLM helped structure the customer feedback into categories such as accepted UAT scenarios, requested usability improvements, resulting Product Backlog updates, risks, and follow-up actions.

### UAT and Sprint Review Summaries

LLM support was used to formulate UAT results and Sprint Review outcomes in clear English. The team used the customer meeting discussion as the factual basis and then reviewed the generated wording to ensure that it did not invent acceptance results, issue numbers, or completed work that had not actually happened.

### Retrospective and Reflection

The LLM was used to polish the wording of the Week 4 retrospective and reflection. The team provided the actual ideas first, such as achieving the Sprint Goal, receiving customer feedback, noticing leftover placeholders, adding tests late, and improving frontend/backend synchronization. The LLM then helped convert these points into a structured academic format.

### Documentation Wording

LLM assistance was used to improve grammar, clarity, and consistency of English documentation. This was especially helpful for converting short internal notes into complete markdown sections suitable for the public repository and Moodle submission.

## Human Review and Verification

All LLM-generated content was checked manually by the team before use. The team verified that:

- the generated text matched the actual Sprint work;
- customer feedback was not exaggerated or changed;
- no fake issue links, PR links, or CI results were invented;
- unfinished work was marked honestly as in progress or TODO;
- private information was not added to public documentation without approval;
- wording remained consistent with the real state of the project.

When the LLM suggested information that was not confirmed, the team either removed it or replaced it with a TODO placeholder.

## Limitations and Risks

The main limitation of using LLMs was the risk of overgeneralization. Sometimes the LLM produced wording that sounded complete even when the underlying evidence was not ready yet. For example, the team avoided claiming that CI, coverage, or automated quality evidence was finalized because this had not been confirmed at the time of writing.

Another limitation was that the LLM could not independently know the current state of the repository, Product Backlog, or Sprint Backlog unless this information was provided by the team. Therefore, all technical and process-specific details had to be checked against project artifacts, screenshots, transcripts, and team knowledge.

## Impact on the Team Workflow

LLM usage helped the team save time on documentation formatting and improved the clarity of written reports. It was especially useful for transforming raw meeting notes and short retrospective points into structured markdown files.

At the same time, the team treated LLM output as a draft rather than a final answer. Human review remained necessary to keep the documentation accurate, honest, and aligned with the real project status.

## Conclusion

In Week 4, LLMs were used as an auxiliary documentation and organization tool. They helped with structuring reports, summarizing customer feedback, preparing retrospective and reflection sections, and improving the clarity of English text. All LLM-supported work was manually reviewed and corrected by the team before being used in the final project materials.
