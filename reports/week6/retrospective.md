# Week 6 Retrospective

**Project:** LAMBA  
**Week:** Week 6 / Sprint 4  
**Retrospective format:** Asynchronous team discussion in chat  
**Retrospective outcome:** Successful sprint, but final handover still pending

## Context

The Week 6 retrospective was discussed asynchronously in the team chat. The team reviewed the Sprint 4 development process, customer-trial preparation, transition-readiness risks, and what should be improved before the final Week 7 handover.

The sprint was considered successful because the team completed important product work and improved the overall product value. At the same time, the team recognized that the final handover is still pending and must be completed in Week 7 after Sprint 5.

## What went well

| Observation | Details |
|---|---|
| Trip mode was implemented | The team implemented the trip mode feature, even though it was not originally expected in the MVP scope. This significantly improved the project and made the product more useful. |
| Development speed improved | The whole team became faster because members were more familiar with the development environment and the project structure. |
| Processes became clearer | Issue-based work, pull requests, reviews, and documentation expectations became more understandable compared with earlier weeks. |
| Sprint 4 produced visible customer value | The sprint resulted in customer-visible improvements that could be discussed during the Week 6 transition-readiness meeting and trial. |
| Team participation was active | All team members contributed to the work during the sprint. |

## What did not go well

| Problem | Impact |
|---|---|
| Version confusion before customer demonstration | The team had problems with product versions and spent too much time finding the correct version to demonstrate to the customer. This created unnecessary risk before the customer meeting. |
| Frontend-backend synchronization was still imperfect | Some frontend and backend work was not fully synchronized, which created integration friction and required additional coordination. |
| Final handover is not completed yet | The product can already be used as a Week 6 trial release, but final transition activities such as final release, customer-side handover documentation, and final customer transfer remain for Week 7. |

## Recurring issues

| Recurring issue | Week 6 observation |
|---|---|
| Frontend-backend coordination | The team again faced some synchronization issues between frontend and backend development. This needs to be managed more actively in Sprint 5. |
| Release preparation risk | Finding and confirming the correct version for customer demonstration took longer than expected. Release artifacts should be easier to identify next week. |
| Documentation follows implementation | Some handover and customer-facing documentation depends on final Week 7 decisions, so it still needs final updates after Sprint 5. |

## Improvements since the previous retrospective

| Improvement | Evidence / explanation |
|---|---|
| Faster development pace | Team members became more comfortable with the environment, tools, and codebase. |
| Clearer development workflow | The team better understood how to use issues, PRs, reviews, and sprint tracking. |
| Better transition focus | The team became more aware of what is needed for final handover, not only feature implementation. |
| More customer-oriented work | Sprint 4 work and the Week 6 meeting helped connect product development with actual customer feedback and transition needs. |

## Decisions for Sprint 5

Sprint 5 will be used as a final follow-up sprint before product handover. The focus will be maintenance, visual polish, documentation, final release preparation, and transition completion rather than large new features.

| Action item | Purpose | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Finalize visual polish | Improve the product appearance and remove customer-visible rough edges. | Sprint 5 |
| Update customer-facing documentation | Make customer handover, deployment, access, and usage instructions clearer before final delivery. | Sprint 5 |
| Prepare the final release | Publish the final MVP v3 release and make the final product artifact easy to access. | Sprint 5 / Week 7 |
| Complete final customer handover | Transfer the project to the customer after Sprint 5 scope is finalized. | Week 7 |
| Improve frontend-backend coordination | Reduce integration delays by aligning API contracts and implementation status earlier. | Sprint 5 |
| Avoid version confusion | Clearly mark the release candidate and final release artifacts before customer review. | Sprint 5 |

## Team participation

All team members participated in Sprint 4 work. The retrospective did not identify inactive team members. The main team-level improvement area is not participation, but coordination: the team should reduce frontend-backend synchronization problems and make release artifacts easier to identify.

## Retrospective conclusion

Sprint 4 was successful because the team delivered valuable product improvements, especially the trip mode feature, and became faster at development. The main risks are connected to final transition: release clarity, frontend-backend synchronization, documentation finalization, and customer handover.

The final product handover is intentionally left for Week 7 after Sprint 5. Sprint 5 should focus on visual polish, final documentation, final release preparation, and completing the transition to the customer.
