# Week 6 Reflection

**Project:** LAMBA  
**Week:** Week 6 / Sprint 4  
**Focus:** trial release, transition readiness, customer-facing documentation, and preparation for Week 7 final handover

## Summary

Week 6 helped the team separate two different states of the product:

1. the product is already usable as a Week 6 trial release;
2. the product is not yet fully handed over to the customer.

The customer can already try the current product and inspect its main flows, but the final transition should happen in Week 7 after Sprint 5. This distinction is important because the final handover requires not only working product features, but also clear documentation, stable release artifacts, access instructions, and a transition path that another team or the customer can continue using.

## Main learning from Week 6

The main conclusion from Week 6 is that “usable product” and “fully handed-over product” are not the same thing.

The current product can already be used and reviewed by the customer as a trial release. However, full handover requires additional transition work: final release preparation, documentation finalization, customer-side operation guidance, and clear ownership/access expectations.

The team also learned that the customer cares not only about implemented features, but also about whether a future team can understand, run, and maintain the product after final delivery.

## Most useful Week 6 activity

The most useful activity was preparing and reviewing the customer-facing handover documentation.

Working on `docs/customer-handover.md` helped the team identify what is still missing before a realistic final transition. It made the remaining Week 7 work clearer, including:

- final release access;
- customer-side backend operation guidance;
- deployment and verification instructions;
- source-code archive preparation after Sprint 5 scope is finalized;
- prepared test accounts if they are still needed;
- clear documentation for future maintenance.

This documentation work also helped connect product development with actual handover requirements instead of treating the final delivery as only a code release.

## Main risk before Week 7

The main risk before Week 7 is transition readiness.

Even if the product features are mostly complete, the handover can still fail or become difficult if the customer does not receive clear instructions, stable artifacts, and enough information to run or maintain the product independently.

The most important transition risks are:

- final release and APK access must be clear;
- backend deployment and operation instructions must be detailed enough;
- customer-facing documentation must be updated after Sprint 5;
- source-code archive preparation should happen only after Sprint 5 scope is finalized;
- test accounts should be prepared only if they are still useful after final development work;
- the team must avoid confusion about which version is the correct release candidate or final release.

## What the team will change in Week 7

In Week 7, the team will fix the release-candidate process earlier.

The Week 6 customer demonstration showed that version confusion can create unnecessary risk before a customer meeting. For Sprint 5 and the final release, the team should clearly identify the correct release candidate and final release artifact in advance.

This should make final review and handover smoother because the team, customer, and teaching staff will all be able to identify the correct version of the product.

## Week 7 focus

Sprint 5 will focus on final transition rather than large new features.

The expected Week 7 focus is:

- finalize the MVP v3 release;
- prepare the final customer-facing documentation updates;
- clarify product access and final release links;
- finalize deployment and verification instructions;
- prepare source-code archive after Sprint 5 scope is finalized;
- prepare test accounts only if they are still needed;
- complete final handover to the customer.

## Reflection conclusion

Week 6 was successful because the product became usable as a trial release and the team clarified what remains before final handover. The main remaining work is not only development, but transition readiness: final release clarity, documentation, access, deployment guidance, and customer-side operation confirmation.

The final handover is intentionally planned for Week 7 after Sprint 5.
