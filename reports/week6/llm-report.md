# Week 6 LLM Usage Report

**Project:** LAMBA  
**Week:** Week 6 / Sprint 4  
**Tool used:** ChatGPT  
**Usage type:** drafting and structuring assistance  
**Human responsibility:** all generated content was reviewed, selected, corrected, and approved by the team before use

## Summary

The team used an LLM as a drafting and structuring assistant during Week 6. The LLM was not treated as an independent evidence source and was not used to invent project facts, customer statements, release links, issue numbers, or completion claims.

All LLM-assisted text was manually reviewed and edited by the team. The team remained responsible for checking that the content matched the actual repository state, customer meeting outcomes, assignment requirements, and public/private information boundaries.

## Where the LLM was used

| Area | How the LLM helped | Human verification |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment 6 planning | Helped break Week 6 requirements into practical repository tasks and Moodle evidence tasks. | The team checked the plan against the assignment requirements and actual project state. |
| Customer handover documentation | Helped structure `docs/customer-handover.md` around access, ownership, deployment, documentation, limitations, and Week 7 follow-up. | The team corrected the handover state to avoid claiming final transfer before Week 7. |
| Contributor and agent guidance | Helped draft `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `AGENTS.md` using the real repository structure, workflows, and development rules. | The team checked that commands, file paths, and workflow descriptions matched the repository. |
| Customer meeting transcript and summary | Helped translate and summarize the Week 6 customer meeting material. | The team corrected the wording and kept only content that matched the meeting. |
| Retrospective and reflection drafts | Helped convert team discussion points into structured Markdown files. | The team provided the actual retrospective/reflection points and selected the final tone. |
| Public/private separation | Helped identify which information should stay public and which should be kept only in Moodle/private evidence. | The team removed private recording links, credentials, exact private timecodes, and private access details from public repository files. |

## Human input provided to the LLM

The team provided project-specific information before using generated drafts, including:

- repository structure;
- workflow and CI information;
- backend setup details;
- Week 6 customer meeting transcript/notes;
- Sprint 4 board and issue/PR status;
- known Week 6 and Week 7 handover state;
- decision that full product handover, source-code archive, and optional test accounts are deferred to Week 7.

## Review and correction process

The LLM outputs were used only as draft material. The team reviewed them for:

- factual correctness;
- consistency with Sprint 4 and Week 6 status;
- consistency with customer feedback;
- avoiding premature claims about final handover;
- avoiding exposure of private links, credentials, or sensitive evidence;
- correct separation between Week 6 trial release and Week 7 final transition.

When a generated draft did not match the required tone or accuracy, it was revised. For example, meeting-related text was adjusted to better reflect the actual customer discussion and avoid overclaiming final transition readiness.

## Limitations

The LLM could not independently verify live repository status, release artifacts, private recordings, customer access, or final deployment state unless the team provided that information.

Because of that, the team avoided using the LLM to claim that:

- final handover was completed;
- customer-side deployment was completed;
- source-code archive was already prepared;
- test accounts were already prepared;
- release/APK links were available before they were actually created.

## Public/private handling

The LLM-assisted public repository files do not include:

- private meeting recording links;
- private exact timecodes;
- customer credentials;
- test account credentials;
- secrets or real environment values;
- private access instructions.

Private evidence, if required by the assignment, should be included only in the Moodle submission PDF.

## Conclusion

The LLM was useful for organizing Week 6 documentation and report materials, but all final content remained team-owned and team-reviewed. The team used the LLM to improve structure, clarity, and completeness, not to replace project evidence or human decision-making.
